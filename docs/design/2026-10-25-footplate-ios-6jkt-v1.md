# footplate-ios v1: an iPhone onto a yard through the edge

Design footplate-ios-6jkt, proposed 2026-10-25. A proposal as it was decided on, not a description of what the code does today; the decision is on the bead.

A yard is one installation of yardr: its store, its crew and its depots. The operator is the person who runs it. A device is one program of a user that calls the yard from outside it; footplate, the native window over a yard for macOS and Linux, is one device, footplate-android on a Pixel is another, and the iPhone app this design is about would be a third, sharing no code with either. The edge is the service at https://my.yardr.dev that stands in front of yards: Kratos for accounts, and a router that relays `/user/*` calls to the yard an account names. A hosted yard is one the edge runs for a person; a connected yard is one on the person's own machine that keeps one outbound link open to the edge (yardr design 2026-10-20-yardr-hosted-050n-one-edge.md). An ask is one question as data on a bead; the person's open asks are their decisions, and each is drawn as a card. A weighty card is one whose approval widens what reaches outside the yard, whose word counts as a person's, or what runs inside it (docs/api.md in yardr, "Weighty cards"). The device token is what a yard answers a pairing code with, and every `/user/*` call carries it; the session token is what Kratos answers a sign-in with, and every call through the edge carries it beside the device token.

## 1. The question

mckean picked native SwiftUI as a depot in this yard (ask 13016 on yd-xr9p, 25 Oct 2026) and agreed Android's screens are still being shaped (his mail 86634): this app follows footplate-android, which is the reference for behaviour. This bead is the design; it lands as a document in this repository and names the beads that build the first version. The design is to name the calls the first version needs and say only where iOS differs from Android's client; to decide how the phone pairs by a scanned or typed code and how it signs in at the edge, with both tokens in the Keychain; the unlock in front of a weighty pick; the project layout, the minimum iOS version and how an iPhone layout runs on mckean's iPad; the simulator gate; how a build reaches his device by TestFlight; and the beads that build it, in order, with the screens waiting for footplate-android's navigation to settle. Left out by the bead: push while the app is closed (APNs at the edge, the same follow-up as Android's FCM), an App Store release, widgets, Live Activities, a Mac Catalyst build, and setting up a yard from the phone.

### 2.2. footplate-android: the reference client

footplate-android's design is `docs/design/2026-10-24-footplate-android-14wp-v1.md` in that repository, and its code has since landed and grown past it, so this design takes the design for the protocol and the code as it stands for the calls. Its layout (`AGENTS.md`) is one Gradle module `app`, package `dev.yardr.footplate`, with packages `api/`, `session/`, `model/`, `ui/`, `unlock/`, `notify/` and `yard/` under `app/src/main/kotlin/dev/yardr/footplate/`; unit tests beside the code under `app/src/test/` with a fake yard, and instrumentation tests under `app/src/androidTest/` that the gate does not run.

`api/Edge.kt` is the one origin every request goes to (`Edge.app()` built from `BuildConfig.EDGE` alone): Kratos's API sign-in (`GET /kratos/self-service/login/api` for a flow, `POST /kratos/self-service/login?flow=<id>` with method, identifier and password, a 410 opening one more flow and no more), `DELETE /kratos/self-service/logout/api` with the session token, and `POST /user/pair` with `X-Edge-Token` and `X-Edge-Yard` and no device token. It follows no redirect, never retries a call by itself, and allows plain HTTP only for a loopback origin, which the tests use. `api/YardClient.kt` is one paired yard's client: it carries `Authorization: Bearer <device token>`, `X-Edge-Token: <session token>`, `X-Edge-Yard: <name>` where the yard has a name at the edge, and `X-Yardr-Client: footplate-android/<version>` on every request, and it makes the calls `me`, `head`, `settings`, `getUser`, `crew`, `users`, `userInbox`, `conversation`, `blob`, `putBlob`, `mail`, `ackUserInbox`, `userNotifications`, `ackUserNotifications`, `userAsks`, `bead`, `flows`, `answerAsk`, `approveAsk`, `approveSetup`, `deferAsk` and `declineAsk`, each a `POST /user/call/<Method>` with its arguments by name, beside `GET /user/me`, `GET /user/blobs/<hash>` and `GET /user/events?after=<seq>`. `api/ApiError.kt` reads every refusal in one order, the edge's `X-Edge-Refusal` header first, then the body's shape (an `error` object is the yard's, an `error` string beginning `edge: ` is the edge's), the status last, into `NotSignedIn`, `NotLetIn`, `NoDevice`, `Away`, `Busy(retryAfter)`, `NotReady(why)`, `EdgeDown`, `Slow`, `Refused(code, message)`, `Down` and `Protocol`; a yard answering `replacing` is made again every 100 ms for up to 30 seconds. Its stream skips blank lines, which is how the edge's 30-second heartbeat passes, and ends as `Down` after 75 seconds of silence, two heartbeats missed.

`session/Follow.kt` and `session/Load.kt` follow every paired yard while the app is in front, one follower each: `load` reads `/user/me`, `Head`, `Settings`, `GetUser`, `Crew`, `Users`, `UserInbox`, `UserNotifications` and `UserAsks`, the stream opens after the head, any end reopens it after the last seq read, an away or down yard is tried again after 3 seconds, a busy one after its `Retry-After` (5 seconds where there is none) and never sooner, a yard the edge calls not ready after 30 seconds and at once on coming to the front, a refused device stops its own yard until the person acts, and a refused session stops every yard. `unlock/Unlock.kt` holds the gate as plain functions: `needsGate` puts the system's prompt in front of any pick on a card whose weight is not empty, except a mapping pick of nobody or of you decide, which writes no identity; run and setup approvals are always behind the gate; where the phone has no screen lock the ask's number is typed back instead; and `pickReason` and `approveReason` are the words the prompt shows. `yard/` holds the account and per-yard records with the tokens encrypted under a key the Android Keystore holds, the QR read as a plain function, and the pairer; `notify/` holds what is told of as plain functions, a burst of 3 seconds, and two channels; `model/Nav.kt` holds the screen shown as state, drawn by a `when`, with no navigation library. Android's navigation is moving now: bead footplate-android-zgny, in review as this is written, makes Crew, Decide and Lines the three places of a Material 3 bottom bar, back from a place leaving the app, and a conversation or a decision full screen with the bar shown again on return. The iOS screen beads therefore re-read Android as it stands when each is built, as the bead orders, and this design freezes no screen layout beyond section 3.6.

### 2.3. The yard's side: docs/api.md and internal/rpc/user.go in yardr

`docs/api.md`, "Over the network", is the contract both phone clients are written from. A device makes `POST <url>/user/call/<Method>`, `GET <url>/user/events?after=<seq>`, `GET <url>/user/blobs/<hash>` and `GET <url>/user/me` with `Authorization: Bearer <device token>`; arguments go by name alone; every call is made as the device's user, who the yard fills in where `actor` or `name` is left out; `GET /user/me` answers `user`, `said_by` and `yard`; a call's body is bounded at 1 MiB at the yard and at the edge; and a client refuses an `http://` address unless its host is loopback, and keeps its token in the system's key store, never in a settings file. `internal/rpc/user.go` in yardr serves exactly those paths, answers 401 without a credential and 404 in a yard with no device, closes a removed device's stream at once, and limits a device to 16 streams (503 with `Retry-After`). Its `inTheClear` rule, as amended by yardr-y1c0 on 25 Oct 2026 and by yardr-d5m3 on 26 Oct 2026, is that `GiveSecret` crosses the edge for one ask alone, the daemon's ask for the one-time code a harness's sign-in page shows, and every other secret is never typed on a phone, while `ApproveAsk` and `ApproveSetup` do cross the link to a paired device of the operator, pairing being the grant and `yardr user device rm` the revoke; `SourceGrants` and `SessionScreen` do not reach a phone. "Weighty cards" names the classes `credential`, `as-user`, `outside`, `code`, `trust` and `reads`, says a client draws a class it does not know as weighty with its line, shows the lines first as `WEIGHTY · <class>: <line>`, and approves a weighty card only with `weighed: true` sent after its person's gate; of the four approving calls only a mapping pick with `AnswerAsk` was open over the edge in the Android design, and the run and setup approvals join it under the first amendment above, and the giving of a login's one-time code under the second. The stream of a device carries every event but other people's mail, and a client reached through the edge gets an empty line while the stream is idle, the edge writing one whenever 30 seconds pass with nothing from the yard, which the client skips.

### 2.4. The edge: yardr design 050n section 3.6

`docs/design/2026-10-20-yardr-hosted-050n-one-edge.md` section 3.6 says the two gates (the edge's Kratos session in `X-Edge-Token`, the yard's device token in `Authorization`, which the router passes on and so can read, a risk mckean accepted for the trial with revocation per device as the answer), `X-Edge-Yard: <name>` naming which of an account's yards a call means where it has more than one, a device call waiting up to 30 seconds for its yard (504 past it, 503 with `X-Edge-Refusal: away` with no link, 429 with `X-Edge-Refusal: busy` when the link is full, nothing queued), the events stream carried open with no deadline and the router's empty-line heartbeat every 30 seconds because Cloudflare cuts a silent response at 125 seconds, an answer too large for one frame carried as a stream to 16 MiB, and a request body over 1 MiB answered 413. The Android design's table of what each answer says to the person (section 3.2 there) is the reading this design carries over unchanged: a refusal is told as the edge's or the yard's by its header and then its body's shape, never by its status alone, because the router strips `X-Edge-Refusal` from a yard's answer and passes its status and body on, so a yard cannot speak as the edge about which credential failed, and only `X-Edge-Refusal: session` moves a phone to sign in again.

## 3. The proposal

The protocol carries all of it. Every call the phone makes already exists, and so do the edge's two gates, its refusals, its heartbeat, the pairing by code and the QR's format, and the 30-day session the Android design settled at the edge, which this app inherits without a part of its own. The phone is a third client of `/user/*`, written from docs/api.md and footplate-android's `api/` as references, with no change in yardr, the edge or footplate. What is new is the app itself: its iOS networking, its Keychain records, its unlock, its Xcode project and gate, and its TestFlight path. The screens follow Android's, bead by bead, when each is built.

```text
iPhone ── HTTPS ──▶ my.yardr.dev ─┬─ /kratos/self-service/login/api  (sign-in: the session token)
                                  └─ /user/*  X-Edge-Token + X-Edge-Yard + Authorization: Bearer <device token>
                                        │  edge's gate: the session; router picks the account's yard by X-Edge-Yard
                                        ├─ hosted yard:    TCP to its container or droplet
                                        └─ connected yard: down the link the yard opened
                                               yard's gate: the device token
```

### 3.1. The calls of the first version

Every request goes to one origin, the edge the app was built for (3.2), and carries `X-Yardr-Client: footplate-ios/<version>`, the version being the app's `CFBundleShortVersionString`. Every `/user/*` request but the pairing carries `Authorization: Bearer <device token>`, `X-Edge-Token: <session token>` and, where the yard has a name at the edge, `X-Edge-Yard: <name>`; an unnamed yard sends no `X-Edge-Yard`, as the edge reads it. Arguments go by name, as JSON objects. `actor` and `name` are always the `user` that `/user/me` answered, sent explicitly as Android sends them, though the yard would fill them in. What each answer means to the person is Android's reading, unchanged (14wp 3.2, second table): an answer that is not a 2xx is read by `X-Edge-Refusal` first, then by the body's shape, the status alone last. In the person's words: 401 with `X-Edge-Refusal: session` is `sign in again`, the device token kept; 503 `away` and the edge's 502 are `yard away`, tried again after 3 seconds; 429 `busy` is `yard busy`, after its `Retry-After`; the edge's 403 with an `edge: ` body is `yard not ready` with the edge's sentence behind a tap, tried again after 30 seconds and at once on coming to the front; the edge's 503 is `edge down`; the edge's 504 is `nothing answers` and a call is not made again by itself, since a `Mail` sent twice is two letters; a yard's `replacing` is made again after 100 ms for up to 30 seconds with nothing said; any other `error` object shows its `message` as it is under the control that made the call; a 401 without the edge's word and a 404 on a call are both `pair again`, both tokens kept until the person pairs again or forgets the yard; and the yard's own 503 on `/user/events` with `Retry-After` and neither body is `yard busy`, the stream opened again after that many seconds (5 where it is missing or unreadable) and never sooner. On `/user/pair` the edge's 403 is never read as a wrong code; the yard's `{"refused": …}` carries the pairing's own sentences, as Android's Pair screen shows them. The Swift error type mirrors Android's `ApiError` case for case, so the two clients refuse alike: `notSignedIn`, `notLetIn`, `noDevice`, `away`, `busy(retryAfter:)`, `notReady(why:)`, `edgeDown`, `slow`, `refused(code:message:)`, `down`, `protocol`. No error's message, and no description of anything holding one, holds a token or the password.

**Not offered**, because the edge refuses them or the first version has no screen for them, exactly as on Android: `GiveSecret` for any secret but a harness login's one-time code (every other never crosses the edge and is never typed on the phone; its card shows the command that answers it, 3.5), `SourceGrants` and `SessionScreen`; `MailPeer`, `Peers` and mail to linked yards; `React`; `Arrivals`; `AddNote` and `TakeBackAsk`. Files are narrower than Android's client today only in what the screens do, not in the wire: the api bead carries `PutBlob` and `GET /user/blobs/<hash>` so the conversation bead that follows Android's image and attachment handling needs no new plumbing, and until that bead the conversation shows a letter's files as Android's first version did, by count, with `open it in footplate`.

**Where iOS differs, and only there.** The client is Foundation's `URLSession`, `Codable` and structured concurrency, with no third-party networking or JSON library, as Android uses its platform's. Four differences from OkHttp's shape, each forced by the platform:

- **Calls.** A dedicated `URLSession` with `timeoutIntervalForRequest` of 30 seconds, matching footplate's call timeout, and no waiting for connectivity (`waitsForConnectivity` is false, so a yard away is said at once rather than held by the system). `URLSession` follows redirects by itself; a `URLSessionTaskDelegate` refuses every redirect, as Android's client does, so the password and the tokens reach the built edge alone. Nothing retries a call by itself; the follower and the screens decide what is tried again, for the `Mail` reason above. Cancellation of the Swift task cancels the `URLSessionTask`.
- **The stream.** `URLSession.bytes(for:)` gives an async sequence of bytes whose `lines` the follower reads one event at a time, skipping a blank line. `URLSession` has no per-read idle timeout, so the follower races each next line against a 75-second sleep, two of the edge's 30-second heartbeats missed, and on silence cancels the task and reopens the stream after the last seq read, as Android's read timeout does. A heartbeat line is therefore not wasted: it is what keeps a healthy idle stream from meeting its watchdog.
- **The background.** The follower runs only while the app is in front (3.3). No background `URLSession` configuration, no Background Modes entitlement and no background refresh is requested: iOS would throttle or end a held stream in the background in any case, and push while the app is closed is left out (4), so a background stream would buy nothing. Coming to the front reads the state again and opens the stream after the new head, so nothing in between is lost.
- **Network change.** `NWPathMonitor` from the Network framework watches the default path; when the path's identity changes (Wi-Fi to mobile data, a VPN coming up) the follower cancels its streams and opens them again on the new path, where Android uses a `ConnectivityManager` callback for the same end. The monitor judges nothing about reachability; it only marks the change.

The wire types are `Codable` structs that ignore fields they do not know, as `kotlinx.serialization` does on Android, with `seq` and ask numbers as `Int64`. The client's origin is taken as a constructor argument, so the tests point it at the fake yard (3.7); the app constructs it in one place from the built edge, and no other code builds a URL from an origin. An origin is accepted only as `https://`, or `http://` on a loopback host for the tests, the client's rule of docs/api.md.

### 3.2. Pairing and sign-in at the edge

**Pairing.** The phone scans the QR that `yardr user device pair --qr` draws on the yard's machine, and the code can be typed in its place. The QR is yardr's, unchanged: `<address>#<code>` or `<address>#<code>#<yard>`. The phone splits the text at `#` into at most three parts, the first the address, the second the code and the third, where there is one, the yard's name at the edge. Text with more than three parts, an address that is not the edge the app was built for, or a code that is not eight letters and digits is refused with the reason before anything is sent: `This code is for https://other.example. footplate speaks to https://my.yardr.dev alone.` What the operator types, and what the person then does on the phone the first time, are Android's steps word for word (14wp 3.3): open footplate to **Pair with a yard**, **Scan**, see the address, yard and code as three rows, sign in unless the phone holds a session, and the phone posts the code, keeps the token, calls `/user/me` and opens the crew place with the head line `laptop · mckean`. **Type the code** shows the edge's address as a line of text, not a field, with a yard field (empty meaning the account's own yard) and a code field; a hosted yard's `/ready` page shows its code in text, so a hosted yard is paired this way.

**The scanner.** The QR is read by VisionKit's `DataScannerViewController`, which draws the camera view itself and hands back the recognised text, so the app holds no capture-session code of its own. This is the iOS counterpart of Android's choice of Google's code scanner over a camera library: the platform draws it, the app asks for no pipeline. The app's `Info.plist` carries `NSCameraUsageDescription` ("Scan a yard's pairing code"), since the scanner asks the camera's permission in the app's name. Where the scanner cannot run — `DataScannerViewController.isSupported` or `isAvailable` is false, as in the simulator, on a device without Live Text, or where the camera is refused — the Pair screen says so in one line and **Type the code** stands, so no yard is unpaired for want of a camera. AVFoundation's `AVCaptureSession` with a metadata output would read the same code with a pipeline the app owns, torch and focus included; nothing in pairing needs it, and section 4 leaves it out.

**The edge is fixed when the app is built, never read from a code**, for Android's reason: the account's password goes to the edge's `/kratos` path, so whatever chose the address would choose who receives the password, and a QR is printed text anyone can make. The app has one origin, the build setting `EDGE_URL`, `https://my.yardr.dev` by default, written into the app by its xcconfig, and sends every request there and nowhere else. A builder who needs a test edge builds with `EDGE_URL=https://<test edge>`, which also switches the bundle identifier to `dev.yardr.footplate.edge`, so that build installs beside mckean's app and never replaces it, and its head row says the edge's host. Nothing typed or scanned on the phone changes the origin, and the phone has no setting for another edge.

**Several yards.** The phone holds several paired yards of one account at the edge, as Android holds them: the edge carries up to three yards per account in the trial, each with its own device token, and one session token serves them all. **Pair another yard** on the yard switch runs the same steps without signing in again. Signing in as another account is done by forgetting every yard first: one account is one person, and another person's yard is reached as a membership of it, not by signing in as them.

**A yard of another account** (amended, footplate-ios-n0vs, 27 Oct 2026; yardr-hosted-d3wc sections 6 and 8, as yardr-hosted-m5nc built them). A yard whose owner made the account a member of it pairs as an own named yard does: the same sign-in, the member's own session beside the code and no device token, from a code that names the yard `owner/yard`, typed in the yard field or as the QR's third part. That is how the edge names it in `X-Edge-Yard` for every `/user/*` call and the stream, so it is the record's name too, and the owner's handle is kept with it. The edge lists a member's yards on its `/ready` page alone, with no call to read, so the handle reaches the phone in the code: a code naming a bare yard pairs as an own yard, and one spelled with the account's own handle (`jo/laptop`) is kept as its bare name, never called another's. The head line and the yard switch call it `lab — member of alice's yard`, and so does every place that names a yard. When the owner removes the membership, the edge answers the yard's calls 403, `edge: the account holds no yard of that name`, before any yard is reached. A follower reads that, on a yard named `owner/yard`, as the removal and not as a yard not ready: it stops and does not ask again, and the app forgets the yard as **Forget this yard** does (the last yard also ends the session) and says `alice removed your access to lab.` over the places, or on the Pair screen where none is left, until the person dismisses it or moves on. Any other 403 of such a yard, the owner's yard building say, is tried again as for any yard.

**Sign-in, in the app.** Signing in is in the app, with Kratos's API flow for native clients, as footplate and footplate-android do it: the phone asks for the email and the password on its own screen and sends them to the built edge's `/kratos` path, following no redirect. The alternative the bead names, `ASWebAuthenticationSession`, is the browser flow in a system sheet, and it answers a Kratos cookie, not a `session_token`; turning that cookie into the `X-Edge-Token` header the router checks would need a code exchange or a token minted at the edge that does not exist, and a callback URL scheme or associated domain for the sheet to return to. The session sheet exists to keep a password away from an app that is not the service's own, and this app is the service's own client, as Android's is. The password fields carry `.textContentType` of `.username` and `.password`, so the phone's password manager and iCloud Keychain fill them. The password is in the request's body and nowhere else: not kept in state after it is sent, not in a file, not in an error, not in a log. A 410, the flow forgotten, opens one more flow, once; a 400 shows Kratos's messages. Recovery and verification stay in the browser, as on Android: the Sign in screen's one quiet line, `Forgot the password? Recover it at my.yardr.dev`, opens the edge's `/account/recovery` in Safari, and nothing comes back from it to the app; a recovery ends every other session of the account, the phone's among them, so the phone then says `sign in again` and asks for the password alone, the email filled. How long the session lasts is the edge's settled answer (30 days, extended while in use, a revoked session reaching no yard within two minutes by the edge's per-minute check); the phone has no part in it but carrying its token, and says `sign in again` on every yard when the session runs out or is revoked, keeping the device tokens.

**Where both tokens live.** One account record, holding the edge's origin (checked against the built edge at every start, and every record set aside if they differ), the signed-in account with its handle (the trait the edge names the account's own yard by) and the session token; and one record per paired yard, holding the yard's name at the edge, the user, the device's name and the device token. Both tokens live in the Keychain, by the Security framework's `SecItem` calls with no library: `kSecClassGenericPassword`, the service `dev.yardr.footplate`, one account value per record, `kSecAttrAccessibleWhenUnlockedThisDeviceOnly` and never synchronised, so no token reaches iCloud Keychain, a backup or another device, and a restored copy of the app holds no readable token. The origin, the names, the account with its handle and which yard is selected are not secret and are kept in `UserDefaults`. No token is shown on any screen, written to a log (`OSLog` included, with the tokens' types describing themselves as `<token>`), put in an error's message or put in a notification. **Forget this yard**, on the yard switch, deletes that yard's Keychain item and says the device stays at the yard until its operator runs `yardr user device rm mckean iphone`. Forgetting the last yard also calls Kratos's logout for the session and deletes the account's item, so the phone holds no credential at all. The simulator's Keychain is the simulator's own and proves the code paths; that the records survive a restart on a device and are unreadable without the device's unlock is a by-hand check named in the pairing bead's note, not run by the gate (3.7).

### 3.3. Following the yard

The `Session` group does what Android's `session/` does, one follower per paired yard: while the app is in front it follows every paired yard, each with its own state, stream and retries, so the yard switch can say what waits in a yard that is not shown, and a decision in another yard is told of. The screens show the selected yard's follower. Three things bind it to iOS:

- **Foreground only, by scene phase.** The app is a single scene; its `scenePhase` reaching `.active` starts every follower, and leaving `.active` for `.inactive` or `.background` cancels every stream task. iOS may suspend the process seconds later, and nothing here asks it not to: no Background Modes, no background task. Return to `.active` performs `load` again and opens each stream after its new head. SwiftUI's `scenePhase` is the counterpart of Android's `ProcessLifecycleOwner` reaching `STARTED`, at scene rather than process grain, which is the finer and correct one on iOS where a second scene must not keep a yard following for a hidden window; the first version has one scene in any case.
- **The heartbeat carries the watch.** The 75-second watchdog of 3.1 stands in for Android's stream read timeout and for footplate's head watch, neither of which has an iOS form. Two heartbeats missed and the stream is reopened after the last seq read.
- **Retries and stops** are Android's numbers, driven by a clock the tests replace: 3 seconds after away, down or slow; the answer's `Retry-After` after busy, never sooner; 30 seconds after not ready, and at once on coming to the front; no retry on a refused device until the person acts; every follower stopped on a refused session, since the yards share it. State leaves each follower as `@Observable` state the models read, where Android uses a `StateFlow`; an event is applied once, by seq, however many a reopened stream brings at once.

`load` asks what the screens need and no more: `/user/me`, `Head`, `Settings`, `GetUser`, `Crew`, `Users`, `UserInbox`, `UserNotifications`, `UserAsks`, in that order, the head early so what happens meanwhile comes again from the stream.

### 3.4. The unlock in front of a weighty pick

The `Unlock` group is Android's `unlock/` on iOS: plain functions first, the system's prompt behind them, one prompt per approval with no reuse. The rule of which picks are weighty is Android's `needsGate` as it stands, carried over function for function, including the 25 Oct amendment: a card with no weight never passes the gate; on a mapping ask (one with an identity) a pick of `Nobody — ignore this sender` or of the last option `You decide` writes no identity and goes without the gate; any other pick on a weighty card passes it, a class the phone does not know among them; and a run approval (`ApproveAsk`) or a setup approval (`ApproveSetup`) is always behind the gate. The giving of a harness login's one-time code is always behind the gate too, since the yard refuses it without `weighed` (3.5); every other secret ask is never sent from the phone, so it never meets the gate.

- The prompt is LocalAuthentication's `LAContext.evaluatePolicy` with `.deviceOwnerAuthentication`, which is Face ID or Touch ID with the device passcode as its own fallback, the counterpart of Android's biometric-or-credential authenticators. A fresh `LAContext` is made for each approval and invalidated when it answers; its allowable reuse duration is never set, so a second approval always prompts again. The gate is friction against approving on reflex, not a key anything is encrypted with, exactly as on Android.
- Its words come from the plain functions `pickReason(option, yard)` ("pick <option label> in <yard>"), `approveReason(what, yard)` ("approve <the command or pack> in <yard>") and `giveReason(yard)` ("give the sign-in code in <yard>"), never empty, set as the context's `localizedReason`.
- Where `canEvaluatePolicy` says the device has no passcode set, so no prompt can be passed, the card asks for the ask's number typed back, as Android and footplate do where there is no gate, and `weighed` goes only on a match.
- A cancelled or failed prompt sends nothing and leaves the card as it was, the choice still made. `weighed: true` is sent only after a passed prompt or a matched number, and never as a default.
- A yard from before yardr v0.15.0 takes no `weighed` and refuses the name (amended, footplate-ios-wv46, 3 Nov 2026). yardr words it by the call: `argument "weighed": AnswerAsk has none of that name (it takes ...)` for a call of its `Calls`, and for the three of its `OperatorCalls` the phone makes (`ApproveAsk`, `ApproveSetup`, `GiveSecret`) `an argument: <call> has none of that name (it takes ...)`, which does not say the name, since a secret might be in it. The client reads those sentences for the call it made, the second only where the names it lists leave `weighed` out, and makes the call once more without it: the person has passed the unlock already, and a yard with no weighty gate asks for no word of it, so it is what the phone sent before the word was added. It sends that yard none from then on, on any call, for as long as the app holds that client: a new one, after a restart or a new sign-in, asks again. Any other refusal, and a second one, is the person's to read as before.
- The framework sits behind a protocol the models call, so the gate's plain functions and the sending rule are unit tests on the simulator, and the prompt itself — Face ID on mckean's iPad, Touch ID or passcode where offered — is an instrumentation check named in the decisions bead's note as run by hand, not by the gate.

### 3.6. The project layout

**A hand-kept Xcode project, no generator.** The project is `Footplate.xcodeproj`, kept by hand, with the app target's folder as a filesystem-synchronised group, the mechanism Xcode has offered since Xcode 16 and Xcode 26 keeps: every Swift file placed in the folder belongs to the target, so adding a file changes no project file at all, and a diff shows the file and nothing else. A generator such as XcodeGen would make the project file itself a readable `project.yml` diff, at the cost of a tool every builder and the gate must install and run, a generated project either committed (and churned by every regeneration) or uncommitted (and absent until generated); with one app target and synchronised folders the hand-kept project's file changes only when a target, a capability or a build setting changes, which is exactly when a reviewer should see it change. Tuist carries the same cost with more of its own. Neither is adopted; section 4 names them left out. The project's settings live in xcconfig files, `Config/Debug.xcconfig`, `Config/Release.xcconfig` and `Config/Edge.xcconfig`, so the edge origin, the bundle identifier and the build number are text diffs, not project-file lines. Swift 6 language mode, with concurrency checking complete, and the app target's code in folders, not Swift packages: one target, no package boundary the first version needs, and a folder split that costs nothing to deepen into packages later if a bead ever names why.

**One app, two test targets.** `Footplate` the app; `FootplateTests` the unit tests, which the gate runs; `FootplateUITests` the UI and instrumentation tests — the scanner, the unlock prompt, the Keychain on a device, a notification's tap, the screens' shots — which the gate does not run, each bead naming them in its note as run by hand, as Android names its instrumentation tests. The minimum iOS version is **18**, proposed here and asked in section 5: it is the oldest version that carries everything the design uses without a fallback branch — the `Tab` API and `@Observable` in their settled forms, `DataScannerViewController` long since available, and Swift 6 concurrency as the default — and mckean's iPad, the test device, runs past it. The app is built with the iOS 26.3 SDK and runs on 18 and later.

**Designed for the iPhone, run on the iPad.** The layout is the iPhone's, one column, the bottom bar, the type roles at phone size. How it runs on mckean's iPad is asked in section 5 with a recommendation: a universal target (`TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1,2`) that keeps the iPhone layout at the iPad's full resolution, the column held to a readable width, over an iPhone-only target that the iPad runs scaled in compatibility mode. Either way there is no tablet layout, no sidebar and no second column in the first version, and the iPad is a test device by hand, never the gate's target.

**The folders mirror Android's packages**, one to one, so a reader of either app finds the other:

| footplate-android | footplate-ios | holds |
|---|---|---|
| `api/` | `API/` | `Edge` (Kratos sign-in, logout, pairing), `YardClient` (one yard's calls and stream), the wire types, the error type |
| `session/` | `Session/` | one follower per paired yard: `load`, the stream after the head, the retries |
| `model/` | `Model/` | one `@Observable` model per screen, the navigation state, the boards as plain functions over state and events |
| `ui/` | `UI/` | the SwiftUI screens and the theme from yardr-design's tokens, Plex Sans and Mono bundled |
| `unlock/` | `Unlock/` | which picks pass the gate, the reasons, the typed-back number, the `LAContext` prompt |
| `notify/` | `Notify/` | what is told of, as plain functions; the local notifications and their tap |
| `yard/` | `Yard/` | the records and their Keychain keeping, the QR read, the pairer |

Beside them, `Footplate/App/` holds the `@main` app, the scene and what the screens share, made by hand with no dependency-injection framework, as Android's `FootplateApp` does. External dependencies: none. `URLSession`, `Codable`, LocalAuthentication, VisionKit, Network and UserNotifications are the platform's; the fonts are bundled under their licence as Android bundles them; the yardr mark is copied from yardr-design's assets. A dependency is added only by a bead that names what it is for, mirroring Android's rule against a library for what the platform has.

**AGENTS.md.** The skeleton bead replaces the Layout section of `AGENTS.md` in its own commit, as the depot's rule orders, with the shape above: the project, the three targets, the seven folders with one line each on what they hold, the two test targets and which the gate runs, and the rule that a change moving or adding a target or folder updates the section in the same commit. This design changes no AGENTS.md itself; the text lands with the skeleton it describes, so the file never describes a project that is not on the branch.

### 3.7. The gate

The skeleton bead replaces `.yardr/test` in the same commit as the project, as that script's own comment promises, with the build and the unit tests on the simulator, needing no device:

```sh
#!/bin/sh
set -e
xcodebuild test \
  -project Footplate.xcodeproj \
  -scheme Footplate \
  -destination 'platform=iOS Simulator,id=7EC91353-EDF5-4E93-B644-46A44BA6CFCB' \
  -only-testing:FootplateTests
```

The destination names the iPhone 17 Pro of section 2.6 by its identifier, which is stable on this yard where a name is not, and `xcodebuild test` builds the app and the test target and runs the unit tests in one invocation, booting the simulator itself. If that simulator is ever deleted, the script fails naming the destination rather than silently choosing another device, and the note on the bead that finds it so names the replacement identifier, chosen from `xcrun simctl list` for an iPhone on the installed runtime; the identifier is a fact of this Mac, not of the app. The iPad is never the gate's destination: the iPhone layout is what lands, and the iPad run is by hand (3.6). How long a run takes is measured by the skeleton bead and written in its note: until then the honest expectation is a few minutes cold, with the simulator's first boot and a full build, and under a minute warm, and no bead may quote a number it has not timed on this Mac. UI tests, the scanner, the prompt, the device Keychain and every shot are outside the gate, run by hand and named as such, mirroring Android's instrumentation rule.

**A fake yard** stands in the unit tests where Android's `MockWebServer` stands in theirs. Swift has no such server in its platform libraries, and adding a third-party HTTP server to the tests alone would break the no-library rule for a mechanism the platform nearly has; so the client is written against a transport seam, a protocol with two operations, data for a request and a byte stream for a request, which `URLSession` implements for the app. The fake yard in `FootplateTests/FakeYard/` implements the same seam in-process: it answers each `/user/call/<Method>`, `/kratos/…`, `/user/pair`, `/user/me` and `/user/blobs/<hash>` with canned JSON taken from docs/api.md's examples, streams `/user/events` line by line with blank heartbeat lines between, answers as the edge and the yard refuse on demand (401 with and without `X-Edge-Refusal: session`, 503 `away`, 429 `busy`, the edge's 403 `{"error": "edge: the yard is building"}` on a call and on `/user/pair`, the edge's 502, 503 and 504 with their `edge: ` bodies, the yard's `{"refused": "wrong_code"}`, the yard's 503 on `/user/events` with `Retry-After: 5` and a plain-text body, and the yard's `replacing`), revokes a session at logout as the edge does, stands for two yards of one account told apart by `X-Edge-Yard`, and records every request, so a test asserts the path, arguments and headers a call carried. The cost, named plainly: the fake proves the client's reading of every answer, and the follower's retries, without proving `URLSession`'s own framing, which one smoke test in the api bead covers against a minimal loopback listener in the test target, run on the simulator and in the gate only if it proves steady there, by hand if it does not. Its ATS path is narrow: App Transport Security blocks raw IP addresses by default on iOS 17 and later, so the listener is addressed by the supported localhost name (`http://localhost:<port>`, never a raw `127.0.0.1` literal), or, where the simulator runtime still refuses it, the test target alone carries `NSAllowsLocalNetworking` in its own Info.plist; the app target carries no ATS exception and ATS is never disabled globally (no `NSAllowsArbitraryLoads`). The `API` and `Session` tests run against the fake; the `Model`, `Unlock` and `Notify` plain functions run against plain state.

### 3.8. How a build reaches mckean's device: TestFlight

TestFlight is decided. Until its pieces are in place the simulator is enough, and no plumbing bead waits for it. What is needed from mckean once, none of it in this repository or in any bead note:

- the **team ID** of his Apple Developer Program membership, ten characters from the membership page, which names the signing team in the project's Release settings;
- the **app's record** in App Store Connect for bundle identifier `dev.yardr.footplate` (section 5), named `footplate`, with mckean's Apple ID as an internal tester, internal testing needing no review;
- an **App Store Connect API key** for uploads — its issuer ID, key ID and the `.p8` file — given as a secret to the yard's secret keeping, read by the upload script from its environment and never committed, printed or noted, as `.yardr/depot.md` holds for every secret.

Per build, the yard runs `scripts/testflight`, a script of the yardmaster's or mckean's, never a builder's, on this Mac: it sets the build number from the count of commits on `main`, archives the Release configuration on a clean checkout, exports with an `ExportOptions.plist` naming the team and the App Store Connect destination, and uploads with `xcodebuild -exportArchive` authenticated by the API key. TestFlight then processes the build, minutes to an hour, and mckean opens it in the TestFlight app on the iPhone and the iPad alike. A build signed for TestFlight installs over the last TestFlight build and keeps the pairing, since the bundle identifier and the Keychain's access group are unchanged, where a change of either would strand the records. A simulator build is separate: its Keychain is the simulator's own (3.2), so nothing carries from a simulator install to a device install. The distribution bead (6) is the last of the chain and the only one that waits for the secrets above; its note names the first build's number and that mckean opened it, and until it lands every bead's proof is the simulator gate and its screenshots.

The follower's foreground-only shape (3.3) is chosen so this can be added without rebuilding it.
- **An App Store release,** its review, its signing and its privacy declarations; **widgets and Live Activities; a Mac Catalyst build; setting up or starting a yard from the phone.** All as the bead names them. The in-app sign-in (3.2) is chosen for a first-party TestFlight app; were the app ever submitted to the App Store, whether review asks for `ASWebAuthenticationSession` is that submission's question, and the change is contained in `Edge`'s sign-in alone if it does.

## 5. Open questions

Each is mckean's to decide in critique, with the options, what each costs, and the one this design would take. Nothing here is decided elsewhere in the document; where a section above builds on the recommended answer it says so, and building on another answer changes only what the question names.

**Q1. The minimum iOS version: 18, or wider?** (a) iOS 18, as proposed in 3.6: every mechanism the design uses stands without a fallback, and both of mckean's devices run past it; a person on iOS 17 cannot install the app. (b) iOS 17: one more year of devices, at the cost of the older `TabView` shape and availability branches where 18 settled the API, carried by every screen bead forever. (c) iOS 26 only: nothing carried for anyone else, and the app is mckean's alone in fact. The design would take (a).

**Q2. How the app runs on the iPad: universal with the iPhone layout, or an iPhone app on the iPad?** (a) Universal, `TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1,2`, one column at full resolution, held to a readable width (3.6): the iPad shows the app as designed, larger, and each screen bead checks its layout there once by hand. (b) iPhone-only, `= 1`: the iPad runs the app scaled in compatibility mode, softer text and a smaller window, and no bead ever checks an iPad layout, at no cost to any bead. (c) A tablet layout, sidebar or two columns: a second layout to design, build and keep, against the bead's iPhone-first order. The design would take (a).

**Q3. The TestFlight identity: bundle identifier `dev.yardr.footplate` and the name `footplate`?** (a) As proposed in 3.8: the same identifier Android uses on its own store, the railway word as the name. (b) `dev.yardr.footplate.ios`, if a single identifier across stores is ever wanted distinct; the cost is only that the two apps' identifiers differ where no system compares them. What 3.8 needs from mckean rides with either answer: the team ID, the app record and the API key as a secret. The design would take (a).

**Q4. The lock screen and notifications: the system's preview setting, or nothing sensitive ever?** iOS gives an app no public version of a notification (3.5), so the choice is (a) accept the person's own preview setting, the design's proposal, the full text where they allow previews and the kind alone where they do not; or (b) the app sends only the kind and the count itself, `A decision` and `2 decisions`, tapping through for the words, which no setting can then widen — at the cost of the sender and the first line Android shows, given up on the unlocked phone too. The design would take (a).
